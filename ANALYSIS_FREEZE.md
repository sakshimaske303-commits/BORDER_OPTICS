## Primary estimand

The treated-only before/after change in NDBI between 2021 and 2025 across my 251-village core sample (H1), tested under both my full-year and summer-matched compositing windows. This is the estimand my paper's headline conclusion (Sections 4.2, 4.9, 5, 8) is stated against. See `BO_Research_Paper.md` §3.12 for my full primary/secondary/exploratory hierarchy.

## Secondary, pre-specified robustness checks on the primary estimand

Control-group DiD (H4), buffer-radius sweep (250/500/1000m), leave-one-district-out, randomization inference (2,000 permutations, seed=42), Holm-Bonferroni correction across my 8 H1/H3 headline p-values.

## Secondary, independent research questions

Night-lights change, border-proximity correlation (H3), three-point multi-year trend (2021/2023/2025), budget-vs-outcome descriptive comparison (RQ2).

## Exploratory finding, now stress-tested (see item 5 below)

The summer-window H3 NDBI-border-proximity correlation (ρ = 0.291, p = 0.0000026), which only showed up after my Entry 22 data correction. As of Development Log Entry 32 I've put this through leave-one-out and randomization-inference checks (and a spatial correction — item 7 below), and it survives all three; I no longer think of it as "unstressed," though it still doesn't feed back into my primary estimand's conclusion.

## Multiple-testing family

I apply Holm-Bonferroni correction across exactly 8 tests: H1 (NDBI, lights) × 2 windows, H3 border-proximity (NDBI, lights) × 2 windows. I evaluate the control-group DiD, buffer-radius sweep, leave-one-out, randomization inference, and multi-year trend on their own terms as robustness checks on hypotheses I've already tested, not as additional members of this correction family (`BO_Research_Paper.md` §4.9).

I then run the two Holm-significant H3 results through a second, separate correction — this is sequential, not an alternative to Holm: Holm-Bonferroni (raw→Holm-adjusted) corrects for me running 8 tests at once; the spatial-autocorrelation correction (Holm-adjusted→spatially-corrected, §6.11) then corrects those same two results again for the fact that my villages aren't spatially independent observations. I report both numbers, and treat the spatially-corrected one as final: summer NDBI-proximity raw p=2.64e-6 → Holm-adjusted p=2.12e-5 → spatially-corrected p=0.02949; full-year lights-proximity raw p=5.48e-5 → Holm-adjusted p=3.83e-4 → spatially-corrected p=0.00350.

## Statistical tests used

Wilcoxon signed-rank (paired, `alternative="greater"`) for before/after change. Spearman rank correlation for RQ2 (budget) and H3 (border proximity). OLS Difference-in-Differences with district fixed effects, cluster-robust SE by district (my primary specification), and a parallel HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust no-fixed-effects specification (comparison), both via `statsmodels`. Mann-Whitney U for my baseline-balance and geocoding-coverage checks.

## Statistical power (Minimum Detectable Effect)

I added this after this freeze, to answer a question a clean null result doesn't answer on its own: could my design have detected a real effect if one existed? I computed it with `src/analysis/power_analysis.py`, alpha=0.05 (two-sided), power=0.80, using t-quantiles at my own study's actual degrees of freedom (not a large-sample z approximation) — n-1=250 for my paired H1 design, and 14 districts - 1 = 13 for my cluster-robust H4 design, per Cameron & Miller (2015)'s small-cluster-count guidance.

| Test | Window | Outcome | MDE | Context |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 (treated-only, paired) | Full-year | NDBI | 0.00802 | Cohen's d = 0.157 (below Cohen's own "small effect" threshold of 0.2) |
| H1 (treated-only, paired) | Full-year | Lights | 0.09086 | Cohen's d = 0.157; 21.2% of baseline mean lights |
| H1 (treated-only, paired) | Summer | NDBI | 0.00531 | Cohen's d = 0.157 |
| H1 (treated-only, paired) | Summer | Lights | 0.07095 | Cohen's d = 0.157; 17.7% of baseline mean lights |
| H4 (control-group DiD) | Full-year | NDBI | 0.01918 | Observed coefficient (+0.00668) is 35% of this threshold |
| H4 (control-group DiD) | Full-year | Lights | 0.13983 | Observed coefficient (+0.03460) is 25% of this threshold |
| H4 (control-group DiD) | Summer | NDBI | 0.01331 | Observed coefficient (-0.00275) is 21% of this threshold |
| H4 (control-group DiD) | Summer | Lights | 0.11792 | Observed coefficient (+0.01798) is 15% of this threshold |

How I read this: my primary H1 design's own Cohen's-d MDE (0.157) is basically constant across outcome/window, because it only depends on n and df, not on the outcome's scale — and it sits comfortably under the conventional "small effect" line, so I don't think H1's null is an underpowering artifact; I would very likely have caught a real small-to-moderate effect if one existed. My H4 design is less sensitive (which I'd expect for a between-group DiD versus a paired within-village design), but every observed H4 coefficient I have is well below even that higher threshold (15-35% of it), so I don't read H4's null as a borderline "just missed it" result either. (These are my district-verification-corrected, 721-village figures — I re-ran `data/processed/border_optics_power_analysis.json` against Development Log Entry 37's control list in Entry 39; the immediately preceding 732-village version had H1's Cohen's d at 0.178 and H4's observed coefficients at 24-34% of a slightly different MDE — same conclusion either way.)
